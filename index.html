    Research Interests

      Professional Summary

      I study how wearable devices can track health in everyday life. My work covers the sensors, the multimodal data they collect, and the signal processing and machine learning needed to turn that data into measurements people can trust outside the lab.

      News

        From signal processing to wearable health.

        My background is in electrical engineering, and I began my research in signal and image processing, working on image encryption and FPGA-based heart arrhythmia detection. I later explored reinforcement learning to improve data packet routing in changing network conditions, allowing routing decisions to adapt to traffic, delays, and packet loss.

        At the CLAWS lab at The University of Alabama, I shifted my focus to studying everyday behavior using wearable sensors and cameras. I have developed models to recognize physical activity and estimate energy expenditure from eyeglass-mounted sensors, and used wearable camera images to study eating behavior and screen use. My current research focuses on multimodal analysis, bringing sensor data and images together to estimate total daily energy expenditure. A key part of this work is estimating energy expenditure during periods when recordings are missing, so that gaps in data do not leave gaps in our understanding of a person’s day. Looking ahead, I am interested in developing wearable systems with embedded AI for healthcare or medical applications.
